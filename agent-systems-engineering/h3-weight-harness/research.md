# H3 — Weight–Harness Co-Evolution — Living Research State

_Last reviewed: 2026-11-05 (HarnessTTT recovered; new Meta² boundary audit)_

**Classification:** repeated harness optimization **also trains the parameterized evolver**, while the agent/task **executor remains frozen**. This is not executor–harness joint training ([H4](../h4-co-adaptation/research.md)). The category asks whether feedback from evaluating harness changes improves the *next harness-editing policy*. See [taxonomy](../taxonomy.md). This file is maintained independently; the human-owned [parent research.md](../research.md) is **read-only**.

## Current synthesis

1. **Evo² is an unusually explicit demonstration of the H3 loop**: an evolver (proposer/reflector) makes harness changes; task execution produces feedback; trajectories update the evolver through SFT and GRPO. Executor weights remain fixed. Therefore do **not** describe Evo² as adapting the executor.
2. **Weight updates should be isolated from larger search budgets.** The strongest H3 evidence is the fixed-executor/fixed-search comparison between trained and untrained evolver. Comparing the *best found harness* alone conflates learning and candidate-search quality.
3. **Co-evolution in this sense is a learning-to-optimize paradigm, not general end-to-end differentiable harness training.** Gradients update evolver weights via training examples/rewards; they do not backpropagate directly through arbitrary Python execution.
4. **Four distinct experimentally studied H3 methods are now identified:** runtime/lifecycle patches (**Harness-R1**, editor SFT+GRPO); procedural skill-bank management (**CODESKILL**, manager SFT+GRPO); tree-structured program evolution (**Evo²**, shared proposer/reflector SFT+interleaved GRPO); and **HarnessTTT**, online **per-discovery-task** proposer LoRA/RLOO learning over executable prompts, tools, skills and middleware. All keep the *target executor* frozen during their qualifying H3 experiments, but learning timescale and editable artifact differ materially.
5. **No paper first released November 1–5 satisfying strict neural-evolver H3 was confirmed.** **HarnessTTT** (October 12) was overlooked in earlier reviews and is now incorporated as a meaningful *previously published* H3 result. **Meta²** (November 3) learns a **textual meta-skill** with **frozen optimizer and executor weights**, therefore belongs primarily to **H2**, not H3. Earlier note: no new strict H3 paper was verified for October 23–29;  The closely related [HarnessAdapt-RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28069) (Oct 20) and [CoHARL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36469) (Oct 27) adapt **executor weights during harness evolution** and therefore primarily support H4, not the H3 hypothesis that learning the *evolver* improves harness edits for a frozen executor. Their controlled comparisons sharpen this mechanism boundary without overturning the existing H3 findings.

## Principal anchor

### Evo²: Evolving the Evolver through Weight-Harness Co-Evolution — Google DeepMind et al.
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13025 ; first available October 2026, arXiv preprint (publication status not independently peer-reviewed).
- **Actors:** learned **evolver** `π_φ`, with *proposer* (edit harness) and *reflector* (analyze trials). A separate **executor** `M_θ` carries out downstream tasks with **θ frozen** during harness evolution.
- **Exact loop/training signal:** Proposer Type A edits the sampled harness; fixed executor evaluates; Reflector chooses keep/revise/crossover; Proposer Type B implements a directive if needed; a strategy library transfers mechanisms across branches. EvoTIDE extracts edits from malformed traces and continues from valid prefixes, then uses selected trajectories for **SFT followed by interleaved GRPO** of the **shared Proposer–Reflector parameters φ**. GRPO execution rewards are **differences in externally evaluated harness performance**, `r_P = V(child) − V(parent)`, `r_R = V(after directive) − V(before directive)`, matched on the same tasks; invalid/no-op edits are penalized, and positive Reflector rewards require trace evidence that the proposed mechanism actually executed. A **frozen counterpart snapshot within each training round** prevents moving-target credit assignment across roles. The task executor's **θ remains frozen**; these are not executor-gradient updates. Source: [arXiv §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13025).
- **Headline comparisons reported in paper:** across tested Qwen setups, Evo² **82.1 vs 62.6** best prior baseline on Polyglot, **48.3 vs 41.0** on SWE-bench Verified, **58.0 vs 56.0** on LiveCodeBench. Differences are task- and protocol-specific; do not compare without matching evaluators/model/search budgets.
- **Isolating the trained evolver:** reported ablation with executor, initial harnesses and search procedure held constant: **Polyglot 61.8 → 78.0; SWE-bench 46.0 → 51.0; LoCoMo 47.2 → 56.5; LongMemEval 57.1 → 67.7**. This directly supports a learned edit policy, but the paper reports **p=0.086 for its SWE-bench ablation**, not strong significance at a conventional 0.05 threshold.
- **Generalization:** one harness is selected on each source benchmark and applied to each of the other two **without further evolution or selection**; Evo² ranks first in **5/6** directions, *not all six*. The failure case is **Polyglot → LiveCodeBench: 44.0**, versus **45.0** for DGM/GEA. For memory, LoCoMo optimization uses **1,500 training questions from seven conversations**, LoCoMo held-out F1 uses three other conversations, and **LongMemEval-S 500 questions** are unseen for search and selection but use **GPT-based judgment**. These are limited benchmark transfer probes, not evidence for cross-domain deployment or zero-shot improvement by the trained evolver on an entirely new edit distribution.
- **Interpretation:** credible proof of concept for *evolver-weight ↔ harness* feedback. Not evidence that an executor learns jointly with its harness; H4 claims require different experiments.
- **Search budget, statistics, and limits:** the primary paper gives task-level inference measures (e.g., on LiveCodeBench **3.6k output tokens and 10.2 calls** for the evolved executor harness vs **6.6–6.9k/26–37** for DGM/HGM), **not a fully amortized total including all candidate-search rollouts, SFT/GRPO and proposer/reflector calls**. It does not provide a complete matched `$ / GPU-hours / candidate evaluations` Pareto comparison against H2 search. Table 5 reports **Polyglot p=.001**, **SWE-bench p=.086**, no listed p-values for LoCoMo/LongMemEval; the stated Polyglot +16.3 pp reflects pre-rounded values (displayed 78.0−61.8 = 16.2). Main best-champion comparisons also combine a trained evolver with crossover and differing search heuristics, so **do not attribute all headline lift to training**. Proprietary-executor comparison selects the better of **two** top Evo² harnesses vs a single top baseline harness. Concentrated benchmark set, verifier sensitivity, uncertain repeated-evolution stability and independent replication remain weaknesses.

## Additional experimentally evaluated H3 lines (updated 2026-11-05)

### HarnessTTT: Learn to Optimize Harness at Test-Time for Scientific Discovery — Yingzi Ma et al. (newly recovered H3 anchor)

- **Primary paper and code:** [arXiv:2610.17370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17370), submitted **2026-10-12** (preprint, *not a new November publication*); [official implementation and released harness packages](https://github.com/SaFo-Lab/HarnessTTT). The paper broadens the known H3 set beyond coding, memory and skill management to **test-time learned harness optimization for scientific/algorithmic discovery**.
- **Updated weights / actors:** one **trainable proposer policy** \`π_φ\` (Qwen3.5-9B or Qwen3.8-27B base, with **rank-64 LoRA**) proposes **executable** system prompts, skills, tool schemas and Python implementations, middleware/hooks and run controls. A separate task **executor \`M_θ\` is always frozen**. The proposer and executor may share the same *initial pretrained checkpoint*, but **only the proposer's adapter is updated**, not the executor. This is clear **H3**, even though the authors call it *test-time training* and training is performed **within each discovery task** rather than learning a cross-task evolver that later deploys with no adaptation.
- **Loop / signal:** each candidate harness drives a budgeted frozen-executor rollout that proposes solution programs scored by a black-box evaluator. For candidate \`k\`, improvement over the incumbent \`b\` is normalized by the remaining distance to a reference \`u\` (with a fallback after reaching \`u\`); a shaped reward caps favorable progress and penalizes failed, invalid or repaired candidates. A **leave-one-out (RLOO)** advantage is computed **within candidates sharing a parent**, without variance-normalization, combined with **0.3 softmax max-sharpening**, then applied only to **proposer-generated tokens** via clipped policy gradients and **KL=0.1** to a frozen reference proposer. **Three LoRA-training epochs per outer round**; no SFT stage in this method. After checking schemas and newly generated code, the best valid incumbent **program** is retained, and a harness change is promoted only if the campaign's best score improves; a second parent preserves a runner-up/diverse component. This differs from Evo²'s **SFT + interleaved GRPO of shared proposer/reflector** and from Harness-R1/CODESKILL's offline-trained editor/manager.
- **Search/accounting:** **20 outer rounds per task**, \`K=32\` candidate harnesses per round with frozen **9B** executor (\`20\` full evaluator calls maximum per rollout), or \`K=8\` for frozen **27B** (\`5\` calls maximum). Thus nominally **640 vs 160** candidate proposals/rollouts per campaign, plus an equal number of proposer episodes (**1,280 vs 320** combined trajectories). These are ceilings/configurations, **not billed totals**; tool \`probe()\` is cheaper than full evaluation but has its own separate quota. Training uses an **8-GPU FSDP node** and substantial proposer/validator/executor compute; comparing only evaluator calls omits generated code validation, training and total wall-clock/GPU costs. The 9B-to-27B scale comparison changes both weights *and* proposal/evaluation budgets.
- **Reported experiments:** across **11** directly compared algorithm/science tasks with frozen Qwen3.5-9B executor, HarnessTTT reports a better selected result than **OpenEvolve using the same executor on all 11**, e.g. **circle packing \`1.172702 → 2.635983\`** (sum of radii, larger better); best reported open-weight ≤10B result on **9/11** and better than the separately *executor-fine-tuned* Finch-9B on all 11. On a **13-task** proposer-learning ablation, proposer weight training improves over context-only editing in **11**, ties one, decreases one (EPLB). Across **14** tasks when scaling and transfer are included, tasks with differing evaluation metrics and seeds **must not be pooled as one success rate**.
- **Mechanism ablations (strongest H3-specific evidence):** two-task comparison of where rewards are routed (train proposer weights / update proposer context / train executor weights) fixes initial seed, model checkpoint, evaluator and **320 total agent rollouts after 20 rounds**. The proposer-training arm performs best and reaches normalized **1.00 (Erdős)** and **0.90 (AC2)** by 128 rollouts, versus context **0.34/0.41** and the executor-training arm below plotted range / **0.26**. These are **normalized task-specific progress scores, not absolute task success**. A separate fixed-\`K=32\`, 15-round objective ablation reports the RLOO+sharpening endpoint normalized to **1.00**, compared with **GRPO 0.38/0.26** and frozen proposer **0.14/0.21** for circle packing/Hadamard. This better isolates training than headline best-champion comparisons, but **each condition is one search campaign**; no reported cross-seed statistical significance or cost-normalized GPU-hours frontier.
- **Transfer:** a 9B-learned *harness artifact*, replayed **without proposer retraining** on five new frozen executor backbones (GLM-5.1/5.2, GPT-5.3, Qwen-235B, GPT-OSS-120B), improves the **eleven-task mean normalized score** by **+1.2 to +13.4 points** (+5.4 average) after equal **48 OpenEvolve iterations** per executor. This is **artifact/executor transfer**, *not evidence that the trained neural proposer transfers its edit policy across unrelated discovery tasks*. One search seed per executor limits inference. Separately, substituting **GPT-5.5** and continuing search matches/exceeds reported cross-method frontier on **9/14** tasks, but mixes executor swap and **additional search compute**.
- **Limits/weaknesses:** headline scores are **selected valid output of one search campaign**, not cross-campaign means with confidence intervals; strong task-specific evaluator and reference score used to scale training reward; baseline differences in original seed programs and in total optimizer training compute; limited evidence of lifelong retention, unseen task-family adaptation or benefit amortized over many tasks. The authors exclude **LLM-SQL/Txn** from the stricter frontier count for contamination/benchmark concerns. The paper describes deterministic tool/static validation and sandboxing; the **official repo publishes orchestration/trainer code and example packages**, but public release is **not independent confirmation** of all originally deployed trusted evaluator, request-log and isolation controls. Inspect executable generated tools, privilege boundaries and reward leakage before reuse.

**H3 importance:** HarnessTTT is a **fourth clean H3 mechanism**, and supplies useful **proposer-vs-executor-gradient routing ablations at the same rollout count**, an empirical counterpoint to claims that feedback should *always* update executor weights. Generalization is primarily **portable harness artifacts across executors**, whereas **Evo²** emphasizes a learned evolution policy for repeated harness search. It strengthens H3's plausibility **within task**, without validating a universal evolver or eliminating costly test-time optimization.



### Harness-R1: Learning to Edit Executable Runtime Harnesses from Agent Failure Trajectories — Shao et al.

- **Source / status:** [arXiv:2608.02276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02276), submitted 2026-08-03, v1 arXiv preprint; [authors' implementation](https://github.com/DeepExperience/Harness-R1). This predates Evo²; it is an **additional independent H3 anchor**, not a new October publication.
- **Who learns:** a dedicated **Qwen3.5-9B harness engineer** processes batches of failure trajectories and emits validated runtime patches. In each engineer-training/evaluation stage the **target task executor is frozen**. The engineer receives **cold-start full-parameter SFT (877 selected examples) followed by online GRPO**, where rewards come from actual same-batch reruns of that frozen target, not just an LLM judge. Candidate patches are validated and execute as sandboxed hooks around four lifecycle positions. During GRPO, **8 candidate patches per training prompt** are generated.
- **Results:** average task success on **WebShop/ALFWorld/DBBench** improves from **44.3%** (Qwen3.5-9B default) to **53.6%** (Harness-R1), vs **46.4%** for the supervised-only engineer and **48.8%** for the best listed fixed frontier editor. **+9.3 pp** refers to the frozen target's task success, *not a weight change in that target*. In separate sequential training, **direct SFT of the target agent** first yields **59.2%** and a **new target-specific, trained engineer** then reaches **64.2%** (+5.0 pp). This second experiment **does not demonstrate concurrent executor–harness co-training**: actor and corresponding engineer are trained in separate stages.
- **Transfer:** on **20 unseen target models** the learnt editing procedure yields **+7.06 pp average** (each target supplies its own fresh failure traces to create a patch; it is **policy transfer**, not frozen-patch transfer). On **1,270 held-out task instances**, pooled improvement is **+8.9 ± 1.5 pp** across three seeds after selecting patches from ten example failures per benchmark.
- **Costs/limitations:** SFT, online GRPO, and target-SFT experiments each use a node with **8× NVIDIA H800 GPUs**; task reruns are expensive. The study compares fixed editors, but training and search expenditures should be fully normalized. Engineer policies may specialize to target/benchmark; generalization relies on providing fresh target failures and validated sandbox hooks. Three benchmarks and limited seeds do not establish deployment-wide durability. Independent replication/peer review not established here.
- **H3 classification:** **Strong, runtime-patch-focused H3**. It is methodologically distinct from H2 frozen-weight program search and from H4's truly coupled updating of executor and harness.

### CODESKILL: Learning Self-Evolving Skills for Coding Agents — Yanzhou Li et al.

- **Source / status:** [arXiv:2605.25430v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25430) (initial 2026-05-25, **revision 2026-09-25**; arXiv preprint). **Do not conflate** this paper with the unrelated latent-skill paper at arXiv:2609.33243.
- **Who learns:** a separate **Qwen3.5-4B-Instruct skill-management policy** learns to extract, evolve and maintain (add/merge/drop) procedural skills from coding-agent trajectories. **SFT warm-start** (LoRA, 12,856 examples) followed by **GRPO** (500 update steps, 3-stage curriculum) optimizes management; downstream executor **Qwen3.5-35B-A3B stays frozen** during training. Reward mixes **rubric/quality judgments (GPT-5.4-mini)**, alignment evidence and **verifiable improvement in downstream execution over a no-skill baseline**.
- **Performance:** the revised v2 reports macro-average task pass-rate gain **+11.03 pp vs no-skills** and **+5.10 pp vs strongest prompt/memory baseline** over EnvBench, SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2. The initial version reported **+9.69 pp**; use versioned comparisons rather than mixing numbers.
- **Evaluation boundary:** the **test-time skill bank is constructed in two preparatory passes over test tasks**. Authors track provenance and exclude a skill if its source includes the *current task instance* during final evaluation. This attempts to prevent self-task contamination, but permits **cross-task transductive information within the test set**. Reported results are therefore **not equivalent to deployment on a wholly unseen distribution with a pre-built bank**; independent replication and a strictly prospective protocol would strengthen the claim.
- **Costs:** the reported training uses **4× H100 80GB GPUs**, ~**20 hours SFT + 210 hours RL**, plus downstream rollouts and a proprietary rubric judge. These costs should be amortized when comparing with fixed prompt-based skill managers. Its editable surface is **skill-bank content/management**, **not arbitrary harness control-flow code**.
- **H3 classification:** **restricted procedural-memory / skills H3**, with a clear interface to external agent-memory studies. Inference-time management and RL-trained manager weights are distinct; **no executor-weight update** is claimed.

### Evo²: clarification from primary-source recheck

The [October 9 Evo² preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13025) remains a principal H3 demonstration. Its Table 5 holds the **executor, parent harnesses and search procedure** constant while swapping the untrained and learned evolver: **Polyglot 61.8 → 78.0 (+16.3 pp; p=0.001)**; **SWE-bench Verified 46.0 → 51.0 (+5.0 pp; p=0.086)**; **LoCoMo 47.2 → 56.5** and **LongMemEval 57.1 → 67.7** (the last two without the p-values in Table 5). On cross-benchmark transfer, the evolved harness ranks first in **5 of 6** transfer directions.

Its **proprietary-model experiment** (GPT-5-mini / Claude Haiku 4.5) must **not** be cited as an H3 weight-training experiment: *both evolver and executor are frozen* there. The dual-role reflection and crossover search changes make that result primarily **H2-style harness search**. The authors evaluate their better of the **two** top-ranked harnesses there, while baselines report their **single** top-ranked candidate, further limiting direct comparisons.

In the authors' coding setup, the evolved Evo² harness uses **3.6K executor completion tokens and 10.2 LLM calls per held-out task** on LiveCodeBench, versus **6.6–6.9K tokens and 26–37 calls** for HGM/DGM, but **executor inference cost alone excludes harness search + evolver SFT/RL training overhead**.

### Interpretation across H3 methods

| Method | What is edited? | Which neural weights change? | Task executor weights during harness optimization? | Main caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harness-R1 | Validated runtime/lifecycle patches | Harness engineer (SFT + GRPO) | Frozen within each stage | RL/search/target-specific compute and patch generalization |
| CODESKILL v2 | External procedural skill bank | Skill-management policy (SFT + GRPO) | Frozen | Transductive test-time skill-bank construction; narrow artifact surface |
| Evo² | Executable harness and cross-branch search strategies | Proposer–Reflector evolver (SFT + GRPO) | Frozen | Selection bias, compute matching, significance variable by benchmark |
| HarnessTTT | Per-task executable prompt/skills/tools/middleware and search controls | Task-local proposer LoRA (RLOO policy-gradient + sharpening, no SFT) | Frozen | One campaign per task, optimizer GPU/search overhead, reference-score dependence; cross-executor *artifact* transfer ≠ learned-policy transfer |

**What H3 evidence currently supports:** feedback from harness experiments can train a **separate editing/management policy** that improves artifacts around a fixed executor; this is not yet proof of a universally optimal harness or model/harness joint gradient training. Compare **fixed-editor vs trained-editor**, **search+train compute vs fixed search**, **held-out transfer protocol**, **retention/regressions** and **sandbox/permissions** before concluding a method is superior.

### Closely related new results that do **not** meet H3 (primary-source check, 2026-10-29)

**CoHARL — [Co-Evolving Agent Policies and Executable Harnesses with Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36469), submitted 2026-10-27 (H4).** The *task executor* Qwen3.5-35B-A3B receives **GRPO/LoRA-rank-32 policy updates** from rollouts over a population of up to **eight** changing harnesses. A separate **Claude Opus 4.7 fixed-weight proposer** edits harness code using the updated executor's traces; **its weights are not trained from harness-edit trajectories**. Thus the moving \`θ_executor\`, not learned \`φ_evolver\`, is the source of neural updates. On 110 held-out SWE-rebench tasks, CoHARL **27.6% avg@3**, harness-only Meta-Harness **21.2%**, policy-only GRPO **20.3%**, base **17.3%**; on 500 SWE-bench Verified tasks, **59.8%** vs harness-only **52.0%** and GRPO **49.8%** (single attempt). Separate **30-step training-budget** ablation: full **28/110**, frozen harness **22/110**, search-then-RL **25/110**, single-active-harness alternating **21/110**. Main protocol: **150 steps × nominal 48 rollouts**, ~**10% extra harness-validation execution** plus separate proposer inference; main training episodes differ by <1% against RL-only, but **total budgets are not matched** and 30-step controls have **7 vs 8 search invocations** in one comparator. No significance is established by the small 3/110 difference in the controlled search-then-RL comparison. This is a directly relevant **H4 counterfactual** for claims that all coupled benefits must pass through a trained evolver; it does not test an H3-trained editor. Source: [main results and appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36469).

**HarnessAdapt-RL — [Training the Whole Agent, Not Just the Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28069), submitted 2026-10-20 (H4).** A fixed GLM-5.3-Flash teacher supplies *mechanism-level training skills* and fixed GPT-5.6 Sol/Codex proposes harness tool/hook edits; **Qwen3.5-9B task-policy weights are trained via GRPO**, using newly collected executor rollouts after harness acceptance. No learned harness-modification policy is established. On SWE-bench Verified / Multilingual / Pro, full **57.6/37.3/39.7%** vs GRPO on original harness **47.8/24.7/33.3%**; the separately pre-evolved-harness-then-RL baseline scores **55.2/33.0/37.0%**, supporting an advantage to *re-evolving tools as the executor changes*, but not to H3. Three evaluation runs, with paired bootstrap intervals elsewhere in the paper; candidate admission is based on finite rollout improvements without statistical testing. Preparation/teacher/harness evaluation costs exist in addition to policy RL; skill guidance is removed at test time, but final evolved tools/hooks remain.

**Additional taxonomy negatives:** [VERSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02616) (Oct 2) changes optimizer *tools/code* while **both sets of model weights stay fixed** (**H2**, despite the phrase “self-evolving optimizer”); [WHALE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00196) alternates updating the *task executor* and fixed-weight harness search (**H4**). Neither demonstrates training the harness-editing evolver as required by H3.

**Newness conclusion:** searches of primary papers through **2026-10-29** found **no new independently qualifying H3 empirical study released October 23–29** and no new independent replication of the existing H3 results. The new H4 results materially sharpen boundary classification but do not alter the H3 evidence ranking (Evo², Harness-R1, CODESKILL). Retain the need for strict **frozen-executor, fixed-search, trained-vs-frozen-evolver** comparisons, held-out validation and full cost accounting.

### Newly relevant H2 boundary: Meta² (November 3, 2026)

[Meta²: Precomputing Reusable Knowledge for Online Harness Evolution in Long-Horizon Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05139) ([primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05139)) contrasts offline A/B/C agent/evolver/verifier-informed rollouts to **edit a reusable textual Meta-Skill `M`**. It then freezes this **external text** and uses a **fixed-weight evolver** to KEEP or EVOLVE a *task-local* harness at online checkpoints; edits do not persist into the next task, and no neural weights are updated. **Thus H2**, despite the word *learns* applied to evolution knowledge; H3 requires an updated neural **`φ_evolver`**. With the GPT-5.6-Terra backbone, it reports **81.00% Terminal-Bench 2.1** and **44.44% DeepSWE 1.1**, with aggregate offline+online costs **$174.91/$367.86** in the sampled evaluation. Retaining per-run edits across tasks reduces scores to **78.33%/39.11%**; valuable **retention/negative-transfer** counterevidence for H3 designs that may attempt universal shared harnesses. Separate Meta-Skills were built for different backbones, so the secondary model comparison is *not* zero-shot meta-skill transfer. This paper does **not** overturn HarnessTTT/Evo² trained-proposer evidence; it supplies a cheaper-to-adapt **nonparametric evolver-knowledge** alternative that future H3 comparisons should benchmark at matched total cost.

**Recent evidence status (Nov 1–5):** searched arXiv primary papers on harness evolver weights, proposer GRPO/SFT/RLOO, and training-from-trajectories. No new *November 1–5* strict H3 empirical paper or independent replication was verified. This is not evidence of absence beyond searched sources. HarnessTTT was first published on **October 12**, so its addition is **research-memory correction, not this week's publication**.

## Related work and classification boundaries

- [Darwin Gödel Machine (DGM)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954), [Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25850), [Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28052) and [Hierarchical Self-Improvement (HSI)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08466) edit agent/harness artifacts with model weights frozen: **H2**, not H3. Even when HSI evolves its *evolver's executable strategy*, this is not *evolver neural-weight training*.
- [Self-Evolution of Model-Skill Co-Adaptation (Skill-R1)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09359) updates skill-generation and model policies under its study design; inspect whether the task executor updates before tagging H3. If executor weights change together with skills, **H4** is more apt. This is an adjacency marker, **not** an additional clean H3 result.
- [EvoHarnessBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04280) measures harness adaptation and retention; it is not evidence of learning an evolver.
- [RLHarness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32326) has **executor** RL updates plus harness adaptation, thus is **H4** rather than H3.

## Evaluation protocol for future H3 papers

For each work record `φ_before`, `φ_after`, executor `θ`, editable harness surface, reward/evaluator origin and any dataset or rollout reuse. Ask for:
- **Frozen-evolver baseline** with matched edit/search/feedback budgets, same executor, initial harnesses and selection rules.
- **Per-task vs cross-task learning:** say whether `φ` accumulates across a distribution of tasks, is retrained on each evaluation task (HarnessTTT), or stays frozen while external text `M` changes (Meta²/H2). Compare cost of retraining `φ` for *each* new task.
- **Reward routing/control:** use a fixed rollouts+model checkpoint experiment comparing trained-proposer, frozen-proposer-with-context, and trained-executor alternatives; even when rollout counts match, compare GPU time, proposer tokens, validation calls and real dollars.
- Frozen-executor confirmation and separation of train vs final held-out tasks.
- Training-specific ablations (SFT, RL, reflection, crossover), variance across seeds and statistical confidence; do not treat a p=.086 comparison as statistically established, nor confuse pre-rounded score deltas with displayed arithmetic.
- Gains on *unseen* domains vs exploitation of a particular benchmark's verifier.
- Cost of optimization and training **including candidate evaluations + proposer/reflector inference + SFT/RL GPU time** (not merely the final executor's test-time cost); safety of executing generated harness code, and resilience to evaluator contamination.
